# Landing Copy: Mechanism (how it works)

This section earns the claim the headline made and the hero section
named. Its job is explanation, not evidence — "here is how this changes
your situation," not "here is why you should believe it changes your
situation" (that's `landing-copy-proof`). A mechanism section that leans
on stats or testimonials instead of actually explaining the how has
borrowed proof's job instead of doing its own.

## What this section answers

One question: **how, specifically, does the product close the gap the
problem section named?** Specific enough that the reader could explain
it back in one sentence. A mechanism explained so abstractly it could
describe any competing product's approach unchanged has failed the swap
test the same way an ungrounded adjective does.

## The algorithm

Paragraph count from the beat count `landing-sequencer` assigned. Order
within budget:

1. **State the mechanism directly.** The specific thing the product does
   — a feature, a process, a discipline — named plainly, in the same
   register the hero section used (no re-invention of terminology
   mid-page; if the hero section named it one way, this section uses the
   same name — no synonym cycling across section boundaries).
2. **Connect it to the problem section's specific friction.** Not a
   generic benefit statement — the mechanism resolving the exact cost the
   problem section named. This is the section's actual load-bearing
   sentence: if it could be deleted and the mechanism would still sound
   equally justified, it's not doing its job.
3. **Land on the changed state.** Close on what's now true for the reader
   that wasn't true before this section — the transformation completed,
   not a segue into proof or a preview of what's coming.

A 1-beat mechanism section compresses all three (state it, connect it,
land the change) into one paragraph; a 3-beat section earns each its own
paragraph, particularly when the mechanism has real internal steps worth
walking through (in which case genuinely enumerable steps become a `-`
list per `landing-copywriter`'s output format, alongside the connecting
prose — not instead of it).

## Writing standard (scoped to this section)

- Apply `landing-copywriter`'s full writing standard.
- **No unnamed authority, here specifically.** A mechanism section is the
  easiest place to reach for "proven approach" or "industry-standard
  method" without naming what makes it proven — if there's no named
  source, state the mechanism as the product's own claim, not a floated
  authority.
- **List-shaped content only for genuine steps.** If the mechanism has 3+
  discrete, ordered steps, a numbered or bulleted list is correct per
  `landing-copywriter`'s output format. If it's one continuous idea, keep
  it prose — don't force steps that don't exist to make the section look
  more substantial.
- **No proof creep.** A specific number, a named customer, a benchmark —
  all belong to `landing-copy-proof`. If a proof point is doing the
  persuading here instead of the mechanism's own logic, that's the wrong
  section carrying the weight.

## Self-test

- The mechanism is named specifically enough to explain back in one
  sentence — not a category noun standing in for the actual thing (the
  same plain-verb discipline from `landing-strategist`'s step 1).
- This section's central claim connects directly to the specific
  friction the problem section named, not a generic benefit.
- No proof point (stat, name, benchmark) appears here — that content is
  flagged to `landing-copy-proof` instead.
- The terminology matches whatever the hero section already named the
  mechanism — no synonym rotation across the section boundary.

## Constraints

- Never lean on a proof point instead of an actual explanation — a
  mechanism section that's mostly a stat has skipped its own job.
- Never introduce new terminology for something already named in the
  hero section.
- Never turn a single continuous idea into a padded list to look more
  thorough — list only what's genuinely enumerable.
