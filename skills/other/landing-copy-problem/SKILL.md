# Landing Copy: Problem / Agitation

This section's job is to make the gap between the reader's current state
and the headline's promised outcome feel real and specific — not to
introduce the product, and not to resolve the gap. That's
`landing-copy-mechanism`'s job, in the section after this one. A problem
section that starts explaining the solution has skipped ahead.

## What this section answers

One question: **what, specifically, is stopping the reader from already
having what the headline promised?** Not a generic industry pain point —
the specific friction `landing-strategist`'s subject statement and
adjective pairs point to. A problem statement that could open a
competitor's page unchanged (the swap test) hasn't located the real
friction yet.

## The algorithm

Paragraph count from the beat count `landing-sequencer` assigned (1–3
paragraphs, per `landing-copywriter`'s ceiling). Order the content within
that budget:

1. **Name the current state.** What the reader is actually doing today,
   concretely — not "teams struggle with X" in the abstract, but the
   specific behavior or workaround the subject statement implies they're
   stuck with.
2. **Name the cost.** What that current state actually costs the reader —
   time, money, trust, control, whatever the adjective pairs' reflective-
   layer sort (Norman) flagged as the real stake. Concrete over abstract:
   a claim like "this wastes time" ships only with the specific
   consequence that makes it checkable.
3. **Sharpen the gap, don't resolve it.** The section's last sentence
   should land on the tension at its most acute point — not a hint of the
   solution. Ending this section on relief instead of tension borrows
   from the mechanism section's job.

A 1-beat problem section compresses all three into one paragraph (state
plus cost in the same breath, sharpened in the closing clause); a 3-beat
section gets a full paragraph each.

## Writing standard (scoped to this section)

- Apply `landing-copywriter`'s full writing standard.
- **Create contrast that clarifies, not drama that manufactures it.**
  "Most AI tools help you generate more code. Hedgehog helps you keep
  control of it" sharpens the reader's picture of what's actually
  missing — it isn't manufactured urgency for its own sake.
- **No manufactured drama.** No "here's the scary part" or rhetorical-
  question openers. State the friction plainly; specificity does the
  work drama would otherwise be recruited to fake.
- **No solution language.** Don't name the product, a feature, or how it
  helps — that's `landing-copy-mechanism`'s content, and pulling it
  forward here undercuts that section's job of resolving the tension
  this one builds.

## Self-test

- The problem stated is specific to the subject statement, not a generic
  industry pain point — fails the swap test if a competitor's page could
  run it unchanged.
- The section names both the current state and its concrete cost, not
  just a feeling of friction.
- The section ends on the tension at its sharpest, not on a hint of
  resolution.
- No product name, feature, or mechanism appears in this section's copy.

## Constraints

- Never introduce the product, a feature name, or how the product works
  — that's `landing-copy-mechanism`'s section, immediately after.
- Never resolve the tension this section builds — ending on relief is a
  defect here, not a bonus.
- Never inflate the paragraph count past what the assigned beat count
  allows to fit in more agitation — flag a genuinely thin beat back to
  `landing-sequencer` instead.
